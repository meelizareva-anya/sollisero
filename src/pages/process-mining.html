Every AI initiative starts with a measurable business case — so the effect is visible in advance.

Teams usually choose what to automate from interviews, memory and intuition. process mining replaces that with observation: what is actually done, how often, and how long it takes.

01

Opinion, not evidence

Automation candidates are picked from interviews and intuition. The loudest pain is rarely the most expensive one.

02

Repetition hides in exceptions

In a written process description, stable repetition and genuine one-off cases look exactly the same.

03

Bottlenecks stay invisible

Queues and hand-offs between people and systems appear on no org chart and in no regulation.

04

Payback is argued, not calculated

Without a measured baseline there is no honest way to say what automation costs and when it returns.

An AI agent takes over four areas. The accountant works with the exceptions.

Standard actions are performed by the system. People keep confirmation, corrections and everything that falls outside the rules.

01

Primary documents

Recognises scans in the accounting system, picks the line item from the reference book or creates a new one.

02

Payments

Finds goods and services received but not paid. Where a contract exists, checks the payment term and prepares a payment order.

03

Invoices under contracts

Prepares invoices, acts and UPD in bulk from the contract terms. Sends UPD and acts by EDI, invoices by email.

04

Reconciliation and debt

Maintains receivables and payables. Prepares reconciliation reports, letters and reminders about deadlines.
